Caring for Toeless Socks

Grip dots are the first thing to fail on a toeless sock. Wash and dry them gently and a good pair stays tacky for many months of classes.

  1. Washing
  2. Drying
  3. Keeping grips tacky
  4. When to replace them
  5. Checking the fit
01

Washing

  • Turn socks inside out so the grips rub less against other laundry.
  • Wash cool, at 30°C or below, on a gentle cycle.
  • Skip fabric softener: it leaves a coating that makes silicone slippery.
  • A mesh laundry bag keeps pairs together and protects the dots.
02

Drying

Heat is what makes grips crack, peel and harden. Air-dry socks flat or hanging, away from radiators and direct sun. Avoid the tumble dryer entirely, or use the no-heat setting if you must.

03

Keeping grips tacky

Dust, lint and floor polish dull the dots over time. Rinse the soles under warm water and rub them lightly with your fingers between washes. Store socks flat or rolled with the grips facing in, so they do not pick up fluff in your bag.

04

When to replace them

Replace a pair when the dots are worn smooth, peeling at the edges or no longer hold on the footbar, or when the elastic around the arch has gone loose. Rotating two or three pairs spreads the wear and gives each pair time to dry fully.

05

Checking the fit

Socks can relax with washing. If the toe opening rolls, the heel twists or the grips no longer sit under the ball of your foot, the pair has stretched. Measure your foot from heel to longest toe and compare it with the maker's size chart before buying the next pair.
